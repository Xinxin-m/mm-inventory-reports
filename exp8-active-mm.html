Exp8 — 10 roster MM2026-09-04

Descriptive, not causal. MMs appear by volume rank only.

Results 2026-09-21 to 2026-09-23 · HYPE · every MM-labeled wallet, 2025-03-27 to 2026-06-30 · Source: Exp8 analysis.md, section 65 · All reports

Who counts as an active market maker around an event

This page defines which market makers (MMs) every event page counts.

  • Labeled MMs. A daily label marks a wallet as a HYPE market maker on days when it places many post-only orders (limit orders that can only rest on the book, never take). 3,130 wallets carry this label on 106,509 wallet-days, 2025-03-27 to 2026-06-30.
  • Active MMs. Around an event, a labeled MM counts only if it both traded and quoted HYPE. Two rules do most of the work:
    • Fill rule. At least one HYPE fill in the 2 hours before the event and at least one during it.
    • Order-rate rule (added 2026-09-22). HYPE limit orders at an average rate of at least 1 per minute, in the 2 hours before the event or during it.
  • Size. On the 922 one-sided surges this gives 97,869 rows (one row is one MM in one event), a median 100 MMs per event.
Definitions: the daily label, the rules for an event, the events and the terms

The daily label (v5d). v5d is the labeler behind every MM count in this project. For each wallet, coin and day it tests four clauses in the order (c), (a), (b), (d); the day is labeled MM if any one holds. A post-only order is a limit order that the exchange cancels if it would trade at once, so it can only add liquidity. Front-end orders are market orders sent from the Hyperliquid web app.

  • (c) At least 2,000 post-only orders that day.
  • (a) At least 1,000 post-only orders over today and yesterday, and post-only orders at least 70% of all orders on those days.
  • (b) At least 200 post-only orders that day, post-only orders at least 70% of the day's orders, two orders less than 1 second apart, and almost no front-end orders.
  • (d) Active on at least 5 of the last 14 days. Over the last 5 active days: post-only orders at least 80% of all orders, the shortest gap between two orders averaging at most 1 second, and almost no front-end orders. Clause (d) has no minimum order count.
  • Second pass. A day that fails all four clauses stays MM if the previous day was MM and the wallet's post-only orders, summed over HYPE and ten other coins, pass a looser test (for example, at least 2,000 post-only orders across all coins that day).

The rules for an event (the population of record). The population of record is the set of (event, MM) rows that every event page uses. A labeled MM enters an event as a row when all five rules hold:

  1. The label calls it a HYPE MM on the event's date, and it is not one of the 306 wallets that reach the MM label only through clause (d) or the second pass. Those 306 count as algorithmic traders.
  2. Fill rule. At least one HYPE fill in PRE2H, the 2 hours before the event starts (t0), and at least one inside the event window [t0, t1).
  3. Post-only rule. At least one fill on a post-only order in PRE2H.
  4. Order-rate rule. HYPE limit orders at an average rate of at least 1 per minute, either in PRE2H (120 orders) or in the event window.
  5. The exchange's order records for the wallet can be read for that window.

The two event sets. One-sided surges (922) are the event set of record: half hours in which one side of HYPE taker flow jumps far above its own previous week and dominates the half hour. Their event window [t0, t1) is the run of minutes in which the surging side keeps outtrading the other. Net-flow events (915) are the earlier event set: half hours in which HYPE net taker flow (taker buys minus taker sells) is extreme. Their window runs from the start of the first flagged half hour to the end of the last.

Terms. Absorbing maker USD is the dollar value of a wallet's maker fills, inside the event window, on the side of the book the event's takers hit. Shocked means the MM's HYPE position changed during the event window by at least 3 times its own typical 24-hour position swing.

Key results

  1. The order-rate rule removes wallets that barely quote, not the MMs that take the surge flow.

    On the one-sided surges it removes 13% of MM rows. Those rows carry 0.6% of the absorbing maker USD and 2.9% of the shocked rows (Section 1).

  2. Shocked MMs look the same with or without the order-rate rule, while the unshocked MMs they are compared with become more active.

    On the 915 net-flow events we recomputed every median in the comparison of shocked and unshocked MMs (position, trading, fills, resting orders, quote distance) with and without the rule. A typical median changes by 2.6% for shocked MMs and by 16% for unshocked MMs, because the rule removes idle unshocked wallets (Section 1).

  3. Clause (d) of the label lets in small automated traders, which we now count as algorithmic traders.

    The 306 wallets that reach the MM label only through it hold 0.24% of the HYPE maker USD of all wallets ever labeled MM. Read one by one, 261 are algo traders and 3 are MMs. They fill as maker like MMs but quote one side at a time (Section 2).

  4. One MM-labeled day in six is quiet.

    On 16.9% of MM-labeled HYPE days the wallet places fewer than 50 HYPE orders or gets fewer than 10 fills. Those days hold 0.16% of MM maker USD, and the fill rule already removes 91% of them from the events (Section 3).

  5. The fill rule also drops MMs that quote before an event but get no fill during it.

    On the net-flow events there are 11,108 such rows, with a median 319 orders in PRE2H. Keeping the order-rate rule and dropping the fill and post-only rules would raise the population's share of all labeled MMs' absorbing maker USD from 96.7% to 98.7% (Section 1).

2. The clause-(d) wallets

Clause (d) has no minimum order count, and its speed test passes for any wallet that places two orders in the same second once a day. The 306 wallets that reach the MM label only through clause (d) trade like part-time automated traders, not like market makers.

  • They are MM on few of their days.

    The 306 wallets are MM-labeled on 15% of their HYPE days and place a median 22 orders a day. We scored every wallet on six simple market-maker tests, such as at least 500 HYPE orders per active day and at least 70% of orders canceled. 5% of the 306 pass four or more, against 83% of wallets labeled mostly by clause (c).

  • They are automated, not front-end traders.

    79% of their USD volume is maker. Their orders sit 3.4 bps from the traded price and live 7.8 seconds, against 74 bps and 4.8 minutes for front-end wallets that place as many orders.

  • Deleting clause (d) would not label them correctly.

    Rerun through the labeler without (d), their 3,028 MM days would become 1,784 retail days and 1,244 algorithmic days. We instead label all of them algorithmic (datasets/labels_v5d_rule_d_overrides/).

  • 261 are algo traders, 3 are MMs, 19 are contested between MM and algo, and 21 are retail.

    The 19 contested wallets stay algo.

  • They fill as maker, one side at a time.

    Maker share is 0.73 at the median, against 0.82 for true MMs. A buy and a sell go out in the same minute on 0.01 of their quoting minutes, against 0.72.

  • They trade in short sessions at a low rate.

    6.7 orders per hour in the market, against 1,514 for true MMs.

  • Other coins look the same.

    28% of the median wallet's fill USD is outside the ten coins, and there too it fills one side at a time.

  • A test could replace clause (d): does the wallet quote both sides near the touch?

    It keeps 83% of true MMs' MM days and 3% of these wallets' clause-(d) days. Adopting it is an open decision; it needs two new daily features in the labeler.

3. Quiet MM-labelled days

The daily label does not require activity on the day itself: clause (d) and the second pass can keep a wallet MM through days on which it barely trades.

  • Quiet days come in four kinds.

    Post-only orders placed far from the price that rarely fill (9,434 days; orders a median 33 bps from the mid, 0.6% filled); an MM active on other coins and light on HYPE (2,401); a large HYPE MM in a slow stretch (1,375); a small post-only trader (4,819).

  • The event rules already exclude them.

    The fill rule removes 91% of quiet days, and the quiet-day rows it keeps hold 0.07% of absorbing maker USD.
