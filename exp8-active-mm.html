Exp8 — 10 roster MM2026-09-04

Results 2026-09-21 to 2026-09-23 · HYPE · every MM-labeled wallet, 2025-03-27 to 2026-06-30 · Source: Exp8 analysis.md, section 65 · All reports

Who counts as an active market maker around an event

This page defines which market makers (MMs) every event page counts.

  • Labeled MMs. A daily label marks a wallet as a HYPE market maker on days when it places many post-only orders (limit orders that can only rest on the book, never take). 3,130 wallets carry this label on 106,509 wallet-days, 2025-03-27 to 2026-06-30.
  • Active MMs. Around an event, a labeled MM counts only if it both traded and quoted HYPE. Two rules do most of the work:
    • Fill rule. At least one HYPE fill in the 2 hours before the event and at least one during it.
    • Order-rate rule (added 2026-09-22). An event rule of this project, applied on top of the daily label; it is not a clause of the label (v5d). The MM must place HYPE limit orders at an average of at least 1 per minute: 120 or more in the 2 hours before the event, or at least as many as the event window has minutes.
  • Size. On the 922 one-sided surges this gives 97,869 rows (one row is one MM in one event), a median 100 MMs per event.
Definitions: the daily label, the rules for an event, the events and the terms

The daily label (v5d). v5d is the labeler behind every MM count in this project. For each wallet, coin and day it tests four clauses in the order (c), (a), (b), (d); the day is labeled MM if any one holds. A post-only order is a limit order that the exchange cancels if it would trade at once, so it can only add liquidity. Front-end orders are market orders sent from the Hyperliquid web app.

  • (c) At least 2,000 post-only orders that day.
  • (a) At least 1,000 post-only orders over today and yesterday, and post-only orders at least 70% of all orders on those days.
  • (b) At least 200 post-only orders that day, post-only orders at least 70% of the day's orders, two orders less than 1 second apart, and almost no front-end orders.
  • (d) Active on at least 5 of the last 14 days. Over the last 5 active days: post-only orders at least 80% of all orders, the shortest gap between two orders averaging at most 1 second, and almost no front-end orders. Clause (d) has no minimum order count.
  • Second pass. A day that fails all four clauses stays MM if the previous day was MM and the wallet's post-only orders, summed over HYPE and ten other coins, pass a looser test (for example, at least 2,000 post-only orders across all coins that day).

The rules for an event (the population of record). The population of record is the set of (event, MM) rows that every event page uses. A labeled MM enters an event as a row when all five rules hold:

  1. The label calls it a HYPE MM on the event's date, and it is not one of the 306 wallets that reach the MM label only through clause (d) or the second pass. Those 306 count as algorithmic traders.
  2. Fill rule. At least one HYPE fill in PRE2H, the 2 hours before the event starts (t0), and at least one inside the event window [t0, t1).
  3. Post-only rule. At least one fill on a post-only order in PRE2H.
  4. Order-rate rule. An event rule of this project, added 2026-09-22; the daily label (v5d) has no such clause. Count the HYPE limit orders the MM places (TP/SL and rejected orders not counted). The row passes if the count is at least 120 in PRE2H (1 per minute over 2 hours) or at least the window's length in minutes inside the event window. The full record page calls it the order floor.
  5. The exchange's order records for the wallet can be read for that window.

The two event sets. One-sided surges (922) are the event set of record: half hours in which one side of HYPE taker flow jumps far above its own previous week and dominates the half hour. Their event window [t0, t1) is the run of minutes in which the surging side keeps outtrading the other. Net-flow events (915) are the earlier event set, called the z_NET events on other pages and the net-flow boxes on the full record page: half hours in which HYPE net taker flow (taker buys minus taker sells), as a z-score, is 3.70 or more in absolute value. They are not the two-sided surges. Their window runs from the start of the first flagged half hour to the end of the last.

Terms. Absorbing maker USD is the dollar value of a wallet's maker fills, inside the event window, on the side of the book the event's takers hit. Shocked means the MM's HYPE position changed during the event window by at least 3 times its own typical 24-hour position swing.

Key results

  1. The order-rate rule removes wallets that barely quote, not the MMs that take the surge flow.

    The rule, our own event rule rather than a clause of the daily label, keeps an MM only if it places HYPE limit orders at an average of at least 1 per minute in the 2 hours before the event or during it. On the one-sided surges it removes 13% of MM rows. Those rows carry 0.6% of the absorbing maker USD and 2.9% of the shocked rows (Section 1).

  2. Shocked MMs look the same with or without the order-rate rule, while the unshocked MMs they are compared with become more active.

    On the 915 net-flow (z_NET) events we recomputed every median in the comparison of shocked and unshocked MMs (position, trading, fills, resting orders, quote distance) with and without the rule. A typical median changes by 2.6% for shocked MMs and by 16% for unshocked MMs, because the rule removes idle unshocked wallets (Section 1).

  3. Clause (d) of the label lets in small automated traders, which we now count as algorithmic traders.

    The 306 wallets that reach the MM label only through it hold 0.24% of the HYPE maker USD of all wallets ever labeled MM. Read one by one, 261 are algo traders and 3 are MMs. They fill as maker like MMs but quote one side at a time (Section 2).

  4. One MM-labeled day in six is quiet, and the quiet days of small post-only traders belong mostly to wallets that are not MMs on any coin.

    On 16.9% of MM-labeled HYPE days the wallet places fewer than 50 HYPE orders or gets fewer than 10 fills; those days hold 0.16% of MM maker USD, and the fill rule already removes 91% of them from the events. MMs light on HYPE are mostly busy on other coins (343 of 568 wallets quote at scale), while 299 of the 656 small post-only traders are mostly low activity everywhere (Section 3).

  5. The fill rule also drops MMs that quote before an event but get no fill during it.

    On the net-flow events there are 11,108 such rows, with a median 319 orders in PRE2H. Keeping the order-rate rule and dropping the fill and post-only rules would raise the population's share of all labeled MMs' absorbing maker USD from 96.7% to 98.7% (Section 1).

2. The clause-(d) wallets

Clause (d) has no minimum order count, and its speed test passes for any wallet that places two orders in the same second once a day. The 306 wallets that reach the MM label only through clause (d) trade like part-time automated traders, not like market makers.

  • They are MM on few of their days.

    The 306 wallets are MM-labeled on 15% of their HYPE days and place a median 22 orders a day. We scored every wallet on six simple market-maker tests, such as at least 500 HYPE orders per active day and at least 70% of orders canceled. 5% of the 306 pass four or more, against 83% of wallets labeled mostly by clause (c).

  • They are automated, not front-end traders.

    79% of their USD volume is maker. Their orders sit 3.4 bps from the traded price and live 7.8 seconds, against 74 bps and 4.8 minutes for front-end wallets that place as many orders.

  • Deleting clause (d) would not label them correctly.

    Rerun through the labeler without (d), their 3,028 MM days would become 1,784 retail days and 1,244 algorithmic days. We instead label all of them algorithmic (datasets/labels_v5d_rule_d_overrides/).

3. Quiet MM-labelled days

The daily label does not require activity on the day itself: clause (d) and the second pass can keep a wallet MM through days on which it barely trades.

  • Quiet days come in four kinds.

    Post-only orders placed far from the price that rarely fill (9,434 days; orders a median 33 bps from the mid, 0.6% filled); an MM active on other coins and light on HYPE (2,401); a large HYPE MM in a slow stretch (1,375); a small post-only trader (4,819).

  • The event rules already exclude them.

    The fill rule removes 91% of quiet days, and the quiet-day rows it keeps hold 0.07% of absorbing maker USD.

  • Clause (d) and the second pass label almost all of the quiet days of the last two kinds.

    Clause (d) labels 51% of the days of MMs light on HYPE (type B in Table 5) and 89% of the days of small post-only traders (type D); the second pass labels 43% and 10%.

How a wallet is judged across coins

New thresholds, set for this test and not tuned on any outcome.

  • Busy MM day. On at least one of the eleven coins the wallet is MM-labeled and places at least 50 orders and gets at least 10 fills on that coin. This is the quiet-day test applied coin by coin.
  • MM day at scale. On at least one coin the wallet is MM-labeled and places at least 500 orders on it, about one every three minutes.
  • Wallet classes, tested in this order:
    1. MM at scale: at scale on at least half of its MM days.
    2. Small active MM: busy on at least half of its MM days.
    3. Mostly low activity: the rest.
  • Most MMs light on HYPE quote at scale elsewhere.

    On 80% of their quiet HYPE days they are busy MMs on another coin, with a median 755 orders across their other MM-labeled coins. 343 of the 568 wallets are MMs at scale, placing a median 5,854 orders on an MM day; 71 are mostly low activity.

  • Most small post-only traders are not MMs on any coin.

    39 of the 656 wallets are MMs at scale. 299 are mostly low activity, with a median 38 orders on an MM day across all eleven coins, and clause (d) or the second pass labels all of their MM days at the median.

  • The mostly-low-activity wallets are largely the clause-(d) wallets already counted as algorithmic traders.

    Of the 315 wallets of the two kinds, 227 are among the 306; 88 are new. They keep 88 of the 101,245 net-flow event rows and no shocked row.

  • A small active MM passes an order count, but not necessarily a market-making test.

    The 373 wallets in this class place a median 265 orders on an MM day. Of the 47 that are among the 306, 39 were read as algo traders because they quote one side at a time.

Example: why a large HYPE MM (type C) went quiet in February 2026
  • It ran out of margin during the late-January HYPE rally. The wallet quoted HYPE only, with $1,000 to $2,300 of account equity and positions up to 564 HYPE. It was short 291 HYPE at the close of 2026-01-25 while HYPE rose 55% in three days, from $22.20 to $34.42.
  • Its equity fell from $2,008 to $25 in five days. It lost $1,071 on 2026-01-26 and $596 on 2026-01-27, its busiest day, with 19,504 fills.
  • The exchange then rejected most of its orders for insufficient margin. On 2026-01-30, 91,035 of its 97,840 orders were rejected, and its fills fell from 19,504 to 597 a day by 2026-02-01. From 2026-02-07 it places 27 to 131 accepted orders a day.
  • It did not withdraw, and it was not liquidated out. Deposits and withdrawals stay under $5 a day from 2026-01-22 to 2026-02-22. Its only liquidation, a 6.74 HYPE short worth $248, came on 2026-02-03, after the drop. A $301 deposit on 2026-02-23 brings it back to about 1,000 orders a day for three days.

Data limits
  • The fill table is incomplete on some dates. It is incomplete from 2025-03-24 to 2025-05-25, and on 2026-04-23 it holds $55.9m of maker USD against $349.4m on a median day. Every exhibit keeps these dates.
  • The daily label counts rejected orders. Its order count includes orders the exchange rejected, for example for insufficient margin. On 9 sample dates from June 2025 to March 2026, rejected orders are 15% to 55% of the MM-labeled wallets' HYPE orders; dropping them would move 2% of the days labeled by clause (c) below its 2,000-order line.
  • Resting-size numbers from population builds made before 2026-09-15 are wrong. Those builds read only part of the order records and wrote zero where orders were not read. The population of record is built with the fix; its files are listed in tables/POPULATION_OF_RECORD.md.
