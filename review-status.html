What changed on 2026-09-17

Fourteen builds of the Exp8 report went live today, letters i to x, each one a patch on the letter before it. New sections first, then what moved inside sections that were already there, then the two data defects found on the way. Every exhibit below is on all 915 events and 915 controls and includes 2025-10-10.

Sections that are new today

  • §4.9, the peak rule as a second shock flag. Reading the peak of an MM’s position against its level at the start of the window, instead of its level at the end, gives 10 shocked MMs per event instead of 5, and events with no shocked MM fall from 63 to 20. Both flags are now reported side by side; the end rule stays the rule of record. §4.9
  • §4.10, what the market pays after the event (test H6). Spread is 1.38 times the matched control during the event and still 1.35 times in the first 10 minutes after it, 1.22 times at 30 to 40 minutes and 1.14 times at 110 to 120. Liquidation cascades are the slow case: 2.05 times in the first 10 minutes and still 1.26 times after two hours. Surge-side takers pay 0.61 bps more in the first 10 minutes. §4.10
  • §4.7b, the equity shock (test H2). The mark-to-market of the shock inventory, on the ten-coin book with funding. §4.7b
  • §9.8, the one-sided surge families against the net-flow events. The shocked share of rows is 5.30% in the one-sided family against 0.87% in its controls, 6.1 times; two-sided 6.21% against 1.31%, 4.7 times; net-flow 5.98% against 1.38%, 4.3 times. The absorbing direction holds in 90.2% of one-sided rows against about 47% in controls, and the top five absorbers take a median 30% of the surging side’s taker dollars in every family. The §8 mechanism survives the change of event selection. §9.8
  • §11.5, the tilt test on all 812 population shocks. Across MMs the lean orders speed; inside one MM it does not: −0.03 with p = 0.52, a per-MM median of −0.08, and only 31% of MMs with the sign the across-MM story predicts. §11.5
  • §12.6, the orders behind the unwind. Cancels run 0.93 to 0.98 per new order, which is record-keeping: the venue writes a modify as a cancel plus a new order. §12.6

Numbers and wording that moved

  • §4 now reads every placement day and every date. Order-state coverage rises to 100.0% of shocked rows, the event count goes 910 to 915 and shocked rows 7,109 to 7,193. The §4 finding holds: absorption-side resting size is 1.14 times the opposite side in the two hours before the event. §4.1
  • §12.5 rewritten on the 35,612 accumulating MMs, with the 6,236 shocked rows reported separately and every short window timed from the MM’s own inventory peak. The 2026-09-16 version is folded underneath as superseded. §12.5
  • The tilt headline is corrected. “Fast MMs do not tilt at all” holds only for the two-hour average. While they still hold the position they lean the most: +4.41 bps toward the exit against +0.56 for slow MMs, for a median of 4 minutes. Table 52
  • Speed is throughput. In the two hours after its peak a fast MM fills 8.0 times its accumulation on the exit side and 6.6 times on the absorption side; a slow MM fills about 0.5 times on each. Nobody crosses the spread to get out. Table 56
  • Two number audits, 128 items checked, 41 cells corrected. The largest: the §4.1 control breakdown moves to the 915-event sample (527 / 218 / 92 / 50 / 28), the §3.3 gate share is 17.8% not 15.5%, the §6.3 hit side is $33.4m not $40.4m, and a §4.3 bullet that compared group medians was false as written (the within-wallet median ratio is 1.01, not 1.14).
  • Sections read on their own. Ninety-five closed “Terms used here” boxes define every symbol where it is used, 218 titles were shortened, 321 sentences that only repeated a table cell were cut, and quantile cells print one value per line. The open text of the page grew 0.2%.
  • §12.1 to §12.4 are labelled as the older tests, so their 30 and 120-minute classes are not read as the current ones. §12.4
  • No 5-minute held rule. Speed classes and the tilt tables use the single-minute inventory peak; MMs back within one minute stay in the fast class.
  • Site menu and hub redesigned. Page titles open their page, chevrons open the section lists, the section in view is highlighted while scrolling, and the panel slides away or becomes a drawer on a phone.

The active-MM rule already handles it: it reads each date’s own fills and falls back to one maker fill when the whole population shows no post-only fill, which keeps 16 events, 14 controls and 3,324 event × MM rows that would otherwise have no active MM at all.
