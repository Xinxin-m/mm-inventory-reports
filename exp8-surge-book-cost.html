What a one-sided taker surge does to the HYPE order book

During a one-sided taker surge in HYPE, the spread widens, trades move the best quote away from the mid, and the quote does not come back while the surge runs.

  • Spread, depth and taker cost. Measured phase by phase on the 917 of the 922 surges that have book data and a 12-hour baseline. Each surge is compared with its own hours before the surge.
  • The best quote. What moves the best quote on the side the takers hit, and whether it comes back. This needs a record of every book update, which exists only from 2025-09-27 to 2026-04-15 (398 surges, compared with 243 matched quiet windows). The measures that read the book only at fills cover 910 surges and 558 quiet windows.
  • Where MMs quote. How far behind the best quote the MMs that absorb inventory place each new order, before, during and after the surge.
Definitions: the surge clock, classes, spread, taker cost, the best quote, MM quotes

The surge and its clock (defined in full on One-sided taker surges)

  • One-sided taker surge. A half hour in which taker buys (or taker sells) jump far above their own level over the previous week and dominate the half hour.
  • Surge side. The side of the book the surging takers trade against: the ask in a buy surge, the bid in a sell surge.
  • Run [t0, t1). The minutes in which the surging takers keep outtrading the other side. t0 is the start of the first run minute and t1 the end of the last. The median run lasts 14 minutes.
  • t_pv (flow peak). The minute with the most surge-side taker USD.
  • t_c. The minute at which the surging side's cumulative net taker USD, counted from t0, stops rising.
  • PRE2H. The 2 hours before t0, used as the baseline of each surge or each MM.
  • Classes. A surge is a liquidation cascade when liquidation orders are at least 10% of its taker USD (47 surges). Otherwise it has a big price move when the mid moves at least 168 bps over the half hour (314 surges), and no big price move when it does not (561 surges).
  • Matched quiet window. A window with the same clock time and length on the nearest day, up to 7 days away, with no surge or net-flow event within 2 hours of it.

Phases (market clock)

  • Accumulation [t0, t_c). From the start of the run to t_c.
  • Peak window [t_pv − 1, t_pv + 2) min. The 3 minutes around the flow peak.
  • After the run. 5-minute buckets out to 2 hours. They start at t1 − 1 min, so the first bucket includes the run's last minute.

Spread, depth and taker cost

  • Spread ratio. The mean quoted spread (best ask minus best bid) over a phase, divided by the same mean over the surge's own 12 hours before t0. The median spread is one tick ($0.001) in every phase, so the mean is the statistic that moves.
  • Near depth. Surge-side resting size within 10 bps of the mid, divided by its own PRE2H level.
  • Effective half-spread. What surge-side takers pay: the USD-weighted distance of each taker order's fill price from the mid just before the order, in bps, positive when the taker pays. Reported as the change from the surge's own PRE2H.

The surge-side best quote

  • Book update. Everything that changes the book at one microsecond timestamp: new orders, cancels and fills.
  • Trade. A book update in which surge-side taker orders buy or sell the whole best level. Taker orders in the same microsecond count as one trade.
  • Travel away from the mid. The sum, over the surge window, of every move of the surge-side best quote away from the mid, in bps.
  • Execution share. The share of that travel that comes from trades. Most of the rest comes from cancels; a small part comes from updates in which takers bought or sold only part of the best level.
  • Quote move from one trade. How far one trade moves the surge-side best quote away from the mid, in bps.
  • Counted book updates. The book updates that move the surge-side best quote or carry a surge-side trade, a median 25% of all book updates. Horizons after a trade are counted in these.
  • Share reverted. The part of one trade's quote move that has come back after a number of counted book updates, or after a clock time. 1 means the quote is back where it was before the trade; 0 means it is where the trade left it; below 0 means it moved further away.
  • Recovered by the next fill. At each fill timestamp where a surge-side taker moves the quote, the share of that move that has come back by the next fill timestamp of any wallet. This reads the book only at fills, so it covers every date.

Where MMs quote

  • Accumulating MMs. Active MMs that built inventory by filling the surging takers, either a large position by their own standard or among the largest quarter of positions in that surge.
  • Absorbing side, exit side. The absorbing side is the surge side, where the MM's fills build its position. The exit side is the other side, where the MM places orders to unload it.
  • Distance at placement. How far a new limit order sits behind the best quote on its own side at the moment it is placed, in bps.
  • Tilt. The absorbing-side distance minus the exit-side distance. Positive means new exit orders sit closer to the best quote than new absorbing orders, a lean toward unloading. The text reports its change from the same MM's own PRE2H.
  • The MM's own phases. t* is the MM's own inventory peak, searched out to t1 + 60 min. The MM's accumulation is [t0, t*). The unwind runs from t* until the MM has unloaded 60% of the inventory it built, capped at 6 hours.

Key results

  1. The spread nearly doubles at the flow peak and is within 6% of normal an hour after the run.

    It is 1.82 times its own 12-hour mean in the peak window and 1.06 times it 54 to 59 minutes after the run. After a liquidation cascade it is still 1.35 times its mean at that point (Section 1).

  2. Depth near the mid holds, and surge-side takers pay more only while the surge runs.

    Near depth stays at 1.01 to 1.09 times its PRE2H level. In the peak window surge-side takers pay 5.3 bps more than in the surge's own prior 2 hours, and 15.1 bps more in liquidation cascades (Section 1).

  3. During a surge, trades account for most of the best quote's moves away from the mid.

    Trades account for 57% to 63% of the surge-side quote's travel away from the mid, against 32% to 35% in matched quiet windows (Section 2).

  4. After a trade moves the best quote away from the mid, the quote keeps moving further away while the surge runs.

    50 counted book updates later it is a further 0.83 to 1.18 times the trade's move from the mid, depending on the class. Between one fill and the next, about a quarter of a move comes back (Section 3).

  5. MMs place exit quotes at most about 1 bp closer than absorbing quotes, mostly by placing absorbing quotes further back.

    Against each MM's own PRE2H, the tilt rises by 0.63 bps during the MM's accumulation and by 1.13 bps in the first 5 minutes after its inventory peak. New orders sit about 11 bps behind the best quote (Section 4).

Which MMs take the inventory, and which surges see the price come back, is on the companion page, One-sided taker surges.

1. Spread, depth and what takers pay

In the peak window the spread reaches 1.82 times its own 12-hour mean. It narrows once the run ends and is 6% above that baseline an hour later.

Figure 1. Quoted spread by phase. Top: the mean quoted spread in cents, median across surges, shaded p25 to p75. Middle: the same spread divided by the surge's own 12-hour mean. Bottom: the surge-side effective half-spread minus its own PRE2H value. 917 surges.

  • After a liquidation cascade the spread stays wide for two hours.

    An hour after the run the cascade spread is 1.35 times its baseline, and two hours after it 1.19, against 1.06 and 1.02 over all surges.

  • Near depth holds at every phase.

    Surge-side size within 10 bps of the mid stays at 1.01 to 1.09 times its PRE2H level, the peak window included.

  • Surge-side takers pay more only while the surge runs.

    Their effective half-spread is 5.26 bps above its PRE2H level in the peak window and 0.42 bps above it in the first 5-minute bucket after the run. From t1 + 4 min on it is 1.0 to 1.8 bps below that level.

2. What moves the best quote

During a surge, most of the surge-side best quote's moves away from the mid come from trades. In a matched quiet window, most come from cancels.

Figure 2. Execution share of the surge-side quote's travel away from the mid, and what one trade does to price and depth. Every book update stored, 2025-09-27 to 2026-04-15: 398 surges and 243 matched quiet windows.

  • The execution share in a surge is nearly double that in a quiet window.

    Trades account for 57% to 63% of the quote's travel away from the mid in a surge, against 32% to 35% in the matched quiet window. The gap is 23 to 30 points in every class.

  • A trade moves the quote further than a cancel.

    In the median surge, a trade moves the surge-side best quote 2.88 bps away from the mid on average, and a cancel 1.22 bps. The trade move is the larger in every one of the 393 surges that have both.

3. Does the quote come back

No. After a trade moves the surge-side best quote away from the mid, the quote keeps moving further away while the surge runs.

Figure 3. Share of one trade's quote move that has come back, by horizon. 1 means the quote is back where it was before the trade, 0 means it is where the trade left it, below 0 means it moved further away. Top: horizon in counted book updates. Bottom: horizon in clock time. Median within a surge, then across surges.

  • 50 counted book updates after a trade, the quote is further from the mid than the trade left it.

    It has moved a further 1.18 times the trade's own move on a surge with a big price move, 0.94 times in a liquidation cascade and 0.83 times with no big price move. In the matched quiet windows the share reverted is 0.00: the quote is where the trade left it. 30 seconds after the trade the three surge figures are 1.74, 1.32 and 0.94.

  • By the next fill, about a quarter of the move has come back, in surges and quiet windows alike.

    Pooled over every fill timestamp at which a surge-side taker moved the quote, 24.6% of the distance moved is back by the next fill timestamp in the surges and 23.8% in the matched quiet windows.

  • Larger taker orders move the quote further.

    Rank each surge's fill timestamps into tenths by the surge-side taker USD traded at them. The quote moves a median 1.04 bps at the smallest tenth and 4.25 bps at the largest. After the largest tenth it is still 2.68 bps further from the mid at the next fill.

4. Where MMs place their quotes

Before the surge, the MMs that absorb inventory place new orders the same distance behind the best quote on both sides. During their accumulation and just after their inventory peak, they place new absorbing-side orders slightly further back.

Figure 4. Distance behind the best quote at order placement, by window. Windows: PRE2H; PRE30, the 30 minutes before t0; ACC, the MM's accumulation; 0-5, 5-10 and 10-30 minutes after the MM's inventory peak; UNW, the whole unwind. A: each side's distance. B: each side's change from the MM's own PRE2H. C: the tilt. Dot at the median across surges, bar p25 to p75; each surge's value is the median over its accumulating MMs.

  • Before the surge, new orders sit the same distance behind the best quote on both sides.

    The tilt is +0.03 bps in PRE2H. New absorbing-side orders sit 11.30 bps and new exit-side orders 11.93 bps behind the best quote.

  • The tilt toward the exit peaks at about 1 bp, in the first 5 minutes after the MM's inventory peak.

    Against each MM's own PRE2H, the tilt rises by 0.63 bps during the MM's accumulation, 1.13 bps in the first 5 minutes after its peak and 0.49 bps at 10 to 30 minutes. In those first 5 minutes new absorbing-side orders sit 0.74 bps further back than in PRE2H and new exit-side orders 0.11 bps closer, so the absorbing side supplies most of the tilt.

How long MMs take to unload. Four in five accumulating MMs give back 60% of their inventory peak within 6 hours, a median 13 minutes after the peak. The slow unwinders are the ones a higher bar cuts off: only 41% of them give back 80% within 6 hours.

Why the once-a-minute snapshot of resting orders gives the opposite sign

On a snapshot of resting orders taken once a minute, the tilt during the MM's accumulation is −13.10 bps (older MM population and peak rule) and −19.8 bps on the same rows as the placement build. At placement the same rows give +0.9 bps. While the surge runs the mid moves away from exit-side orders placed earlier, so those orders end up far from the best quote without the MM moving them. The snapshot records where old orders sit, not where MMs place new ones.

What this does not establish
  • The book is rebuilt from exchange records. Its ordering of events within one microsecond is not the exchange's documented ordering, and a fill and a new order at the same microsecond cannot be separated.
  • Section 2 and the first finding of Section 3 cover 398 of the 922 surges (43%). Only 2025-09-27 to 2026-04-15 stores every book update. The next-fill findings read the book at fill timestamps and cover every date.
  • Set-up problem. The quiet windows of Sections 2 and 3 were matched to an earlier catalogue, the 915 net-flow events, not to these surges, and their class counts are that catalogue's. A set matched to the surges should replace them.
  • Set-up problem. In Sections 2 and 3 the surge window ends at t1 − 1 min, one minute short of the run, while the quiet windows use their full length. In Figure 1 the peak window overlaps the accumulation phase, so the first two phases share minutes.
  • Set-up problem: the event-level unwind window [t_c, t1) is not used. t_c is the run's last minute on 76.5% of surges, so the window is 1 minute long there and holds almost none of the MMs' unwinding. Every result built on it has been deleted. The event-level unwind will be the 2 hours from t_c, in buckets of 0-5, 5-10, 10-30, 30-60 and 60-120 minutes. No exhibit on this page uses it.
  • Every comparison is descriptive, against a matched quiet window or the surge's own past.
