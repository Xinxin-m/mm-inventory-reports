64. What a one-sided taker surge costs the book

A one-sided taker surge widens the HYPE spread to 1.8 times the event's own 12-hour baseline at the peak minute, and an hour after the surge ends the spread is still 6% wider than that baseline. After a liquidation cascade it is 35% wider at the same point and 20% wider two hours out. Executions rather than cancellations do most of the work: they cause 52% to 59% of the best quote's travel away from the mid during a surge, against 35% in a matched quiet window. Counted between fills, the only grain available on every date, the surge still sits 9.5 to 13.5 points above its control in each calendar period. In event time the push stands. Fifty book events after an execution moved the quote, 0.4% of that move has reverted on a big price move, and inside a liquidation cascade the quote has travelled 1.7 times the original push further out.

Setup: populations, sources and compute

Populations. The 922 one-sided taker surges, tables/redef4_events_F1_one_sided.csv, 2025-03-30 to 2026-06-29, classes 561 no big price move, 314 big price move, 47 liquidation cascade. The 915 net-flow boxes, tables/redef2_imb_events_recommended_absgate.csv, appear as a comparison row in Tables 3 and 7. Matched controls are one window per event at the same clock time on the nearest qualifying day, tables/redef2_pop_control_windows.csv.

Per-market-maker rows. tables/redef2_pop_event_mm_F1.csv, 118,269 (event x market maker) rows over 922 events, of which 6,173 carry an accumulation and unwind phase. The comparison file is tables/redef2_pop_event_mm.csv, 126,846 rows over 915 events, controls excluded.

Builders. code/f1r0920_reduce_existing.py, code/f1r0921_anchors_and_tilt.py, code/f1r0921_spread_phases.py, code/tick0920_bookevent_build.py with code/tick0920_build.sbatch, code/tick0921_fillgrain_build.py with code/tick0921_fillgrain.sbatch, code/d0921_resign_skew_figures.py, code/f1r0921_tables.py, code/f1r0921_figures.py. 64.1 Symbols and sign convention

Table 1. Symbols used on this page.

symbol meaning
t_s surge onset, the first minute of the one-sided run
t_pv flow-intensity peak, the minute of heaviest surge-side taker flow
t_c pressure peak, the minute at which cumulative in-direction net taker USD counted from t_s stops rising
t_e surge end, exclusive
t_pp price extreme, the minute of the furthest mid inside the surge
t* one market maker's own inventory peak, the minute of its extreme signed position in the accumulation direction
PRE2H, PRE6H, PRE12H the event's own 2, 6 and 12 hours before t_s, used as baselines
SAME the absorption side, whose maker fills built the position
OPP the exit side
a_pre, a_post the best quote on a side immediately before and immediately after every event at one book timestamp

Every signed quantity on this page is oriented so that the direction we expect is positive. A market maker unwinding inventory is expected to bring its exit quote nearer than its absorption quote, so

tilt_bps  = d(SAME) - d(OPP)        positive = the exit quote sits nearer the touch
tilt_size = size(OPP) - size(SAME)  positive = more size rests on the exit side

Both are positive when the market maker is tilted toward getting out.1

Both windows run through section 64.4 as ACC_c and ACC_pv. The prose quotes t_c, and every later exhibit on the one-sided surges ends the market-level accumulation window at t_c. Each market maker's own phases still run off its own inventory peak t*.

Market makers quote 13 bps away from the exit while the position builds, then turn 1.6 bps toward it during the unwind, and the turn comes from the absorption quote stepping back rather than the exit quote coming in.

64.5 What moves the best quote

Executions cause half to three-fifths of the best quote's travel away from the mid during a surge, against a third in a matched quiet window. The single-episode reading in sections 28 and 30 of analysis.md, 93.5%, was measured at the same book-event grain and is a property of the most intense interval of one December 2025 episode.

Setup: the measure, and why the grain matters

One tick is one book-event timestamp. On the surge side, with m the ts_bookdiff_lt mid and the sign chosen so that away from the mid is positive:

c_bps = 1e4 * s * (a_post - a_pre) / m      what the events at this timestamp did
r_bps = 1e4 * s * (a_next - a_post) / m     what happened before the next book event

A tick with c > 0 is classified trade when a surge-side taker at that timestamp covered the whole a_pre level (or reached two or more levels), mixed when a taker was present but did not cover it, and cancel when no taker was present. A tick with c < 0 is a post, a quote arriving inside the previous best.

r_bps is zero by construction at this grain, because lt(t_i+1) is the same book state as le(t_i) when consecutive rows are the book events. Measured, it is non-zero on 0.03% to 0.11% of ticks and is kept only as a validation column. Replenishment here is the next post tick, not a gap between two ticks. The a_post to a_next gap carries information only when the grain is coarser than the book events, which is the fill grain of section 64.6.

Outside the range the tables are keyed at fill microseconds, so "the next row" means something else and the two cannot be pooled; section 64.6 measures every date at fill grain instead.

64.6 The same measure between fills, on every date

Counted between consecutive fills, executions cause 73% of the best quote's travel away from the mid during a surge and 60% in a matched quiet window, across every date. The surge sits 9.5 to 13.5 points above its control in each of three calendar periods, so the surge effect of section 64.5 holds on the 57% of events that the book-event grain cannot reach. The level depends on the grain: on the same events, counting between fills raises the execution share by 12 points on the surges and 17 on the controls.

Setup: the fill grain, and how it relates to the book-event grain

Outside 2025-09-27 to 2026-04-15 the phase tables hold a row only where a fill occurred. On all 244 such dates used here, every key is a taker or maker fill timestamp and no fill timestamp is missing from the table. A tick at fill grain is one fill timestamp:

c_bps = 1e4 * s * (le(t_i)   - lt(t_i)) / m     the fills and cancels at fill timestamp t_i
r_bps = 1e4 * s * (lt(t_i+1) - le(t_i)) / m     every placement and cancel before the next fill

Gross travel away from the mid is the positive part of c plus the positive part of r. The execution share is the travel from trade ticks over that total, and the between-fill share is the positive part of r over the same total. A tick is trade when the executed surge-side size at that timestamp covers the whole lt level or the taker reached two levels. Executed size is the larger of the taker size consuming that side and the maker size resting on it, because on 2025-03-24 to 2025-05-25 one of the two row sets is incomplete.

Inside the range the same code subsets the book-event rows to the fill timestamps, which gives the same object, and computes the book-event grain beside it with the same classification. At book-event grain it reproduces tables/tick0920_event_decomp.csv on all 3,109 (event x side x phase) units, with a largest difference of 1e-16.

Why the grain moves the level. A cancel that pulls the best quote away and a placement that restores it before the next fill both count as travel at book-event grain, and they net to zero between two fills. Counting between fills removes that round trip. Gross travel falls to 0.81 of its book-event value on the surges and to 0.67 on the controls, whose quotes flicker more, so the control's execution share rises more.

Scope. 910 of the 922 surges, all 915 boxes and all 558 control windows, 71.9 million tick rows over both grains. The 12 surges without a unit are zero-length in the catalogue (t_s = t_e), so they have no surge window. Compute: one Slurm batch job on soal, 256 GB and 16 CPUs, 2026-09-21, 9 minutes wall, 14 GB peak memory, code/tick0921_fillgrain_build.py with code/tick0921_fillgrain.sbatch.

64.8 Where this sits in the order-book resiliency literature

Lining up on aggressive orders and tracking the spread, the best quote and the depth over the following book events is the design of Biais, Hillion and Spatt (1995) and of Degryse, de Jong, van Ravenswaaij and Wuyts (2005). Large (2007) is the hazard-model version, which estimates a replenishment intensity rather than a level path and is the natural next step if the question becomes how long resilience takes rather than how much is restored.

Three choices here follow that literature. Horizons are counted in book events first and clock time second, because the arrival rate itself changes during a surge. Prints are ranked into deciles within their own event, so the comparison is between prints of the same episode. And every number carries a matched quiet-window benchmark, because the level alone has no meaning.

64.9 The book tables

ts_bookdiff_lt is a reconstruction of the book strictly before every event at a timestamp; its best quotes match the last raw Tardis snapshot on 55% to 85% of rows, and its row grain changes by date.

What was verified, and on which days

Three phase tables exist for HYPE. ts_bookdiff_lt is the book strictly before every event at a timestamp. ts_bookdiff without a suffix is after arrivals and updates but before fills, so it is not a post-trade book. ts_bookdiff_le is after fills and then cancellations, and it is the post-trade state. On 2025-12-17, at taker-fill microseconds, lt and the middle phase agree on 87.2% of best prices while the middle phase and le differ on 65% of best-ask sizes.

The row grain changes by date. On 2025-09-27 to 2026-04-15 all three are keyed at every book event: 895,128 rows on 2025-12-17 against 116,988 taker-fill microseconds, and 100% of the extra timestamps are order placement or status timestamps. Outside that range they are keyed at fill microseconds only; on all 244 out-of-range dates used in section 64.6, every key is a taker or maker fill timestamp. Storage differs too, (timestamp, state_id) joined to ts_bookdiff_states inside the range and 20 inline price and amount columns outside it.

ts_bookdiff_lt was never overwritten by the forder work. The 2026-07-30 ordered walker replaced only p_mid in grouped_trade_oid and grouped_trade_oid_leverage on 201 dates; restore_legacy_pmid.py reverted it and archived the bad values as pmid_ordered_invalid_2025-09-27_2026-04-15/. 64.10 What this does not establish

  • The book tables are unsafe timestamp proxies. The 2026-08-03 correction demoted every ts_bookdiff* output because the builder's tie-break, arrivals then fills then terminal statuses, is not HyperCore's documented action ordering. Every tick number on this page inherits that.
  • A tick carrying a fill and a placement at the same microsecond cannot be split. The execution share in section 64.5 is therefore a lower bound on how mixed the ticks really are.
  • The book-event numbers cover 43% of each catalogue. Section 64.6 carries the surge-over-control comparison to every date at fill grain, a coarser measure whose level runs 12 to 17 points higher on the same events.
  • Every comparison is descriptive, against a matched window or against the event's own past.
  • The quote tilt records where orders sat, not where they were placed. A minute-snapshot distance moves on its own when takers consume the levels in front of a standing quote. The placement-time measure, distance to the touch at each order's own order_timestamp, does not exist yet.
  • The unwind tilt is a window average. The standing rule is to split the unwind by the share of the peak excursion still held, 100-75, 75-50, 50-25 and 25-0%, because a two-hour average hides a lean that lasts fifteen minutes. That split is not built for this catalogue.
  • The class ordering in section 64.5 rests on 35 cascade units and overlapping interquartile ranges.
