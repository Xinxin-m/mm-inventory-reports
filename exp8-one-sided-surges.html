Exp8 — 10 roster MM2026-09-04

Descriptive, not causal. MMs appear by volume rank only.

Results 2026-09-10 to 2026-09-22 · HYPE · 922 one-sided taker surges, 2025-03-27 to 2026-06-30 · Source: Exp8 analysis.md, sections 38, 41, 43, 55, 70, 71 and 75 · All reports

One-sided taker surges on HYPE: the events and the market makers they load

We study 922 one-sided taker surges on HYPE perpetuals, 2025-03-27 to 2026-06-30 (349 dates).

  • Surge. A half hour in which taker buying (or taker selling) jumps far above its level of the previous week and is at least half of all taker volume. 439 are buy surges and 483 are sell surges.
  • Run [t0, t1). The consecutive minutes around that half hour in which the surging side's taker USD clearly exceeds the other side's. t0 is the start of the first minute and t1 the end of the last. The median run lasts 14 minutes.
  • Active MMs. For each surge we take every wallet labeled a HYPE market maker (MM) that day that also traded and quoted around the surge: a median 100 per surge.
  • Shocked MM. An active MM whose HYPE position changes during the run by at least 3 times its normal variability (sig24, defined below).
  • Reversal quintiles Q1 to Q5. R(120) is the mid-price move from t1 to 120 minutes later, in bps, positive when the price moves back against the surge. We sort the 922 surges by R(120) and cut them into five equal groups of about 184 surges. Q1 (R(120) below −84 bps) kept moving in the surge direction; Q5 (above +191 bps) came back the most.
Definitions: the event rule, the windows, the MM population, the price patterns

The event rule

  • Side score. For each 30-minute bin, each side's taker USD is scored against the 336 bins (7 days) before it: z = (V − median) / max(1.4826 · MAD, $25,000).
  • Surge. A side surges when its score reaches 3.81 (buys) or 3.89 (sells). These cutoffs give about as many events (922) as the older net-flow rule (915), which scores buys minus sells instead of each side alone.
  • One-sided. The surging side carries at least half of the bin's taker USD and at least twice what the other side traded in the bin before. Flagged bins close together merge into one flagged half hour (the "box"), which is the event.

The windows

  • t0, t1. The run is the stretch of minutes around the box in which the surging side's taker USD minus the other side's stays above a quarter of its 95th percentile over the prior 6 hours (gaps up to 3 minutes bridged). t0 opens its first minute and t1 closes its last.
  • Anchors inside the run. t_pv is the minute with the most surge-side taker USD. t_c is the minute at which cumulative net taker flow since t0 peaks. t_pp is the minute of the largest mid move in the surge direction, searched to t1 + 15 min. p0 is the mid at t0.
  • PRE2H, POST. PRE2H is the 2 hours before t0; POST the 2 hours after t1.
  • Control window. For each surge, the same clock time and length on the nearest day (up to 7 days away) with no surge and no net-flow event within 2 hours, never on 2025-10-10. Controls are not matched on volume or volatility.

The MM population

  • Active MM. A wallet the daily label (v5d) calls a HYPE market maker that day, with a HYPE fill in PRE2H and one in the run, a post-only fill in PRE2H, and at least 1 HYPE limit order per minute in PRE2H or in the run. The 306 wallets whose MM label rests only on clause (d) of the label count as algorithmic traders, not MMs. One row is one active MM in one surge: 97,869 rows in all.
  • Own scale sig24. How far the MM's HYPE position normally strays from its own 6-hour rolling median over the 24 hours before t0 (a robust standard deviation), with a floor of $12,500 of HYPE.
  • Shocked. The MM's HYPE position changed by at least 3 sig24 (so at least $37,500) from t0 to t1.
  • Absorption side, exit side. The absorption side is the side of the book the surging takers hit (the ask in a buy surge). An MM filled there builds a position against the flow: it sells into a buy surge and buys into a sell surge. The exit side is the other side, where the MM can close that position.
  • Absorber. An active MM whose position moved at least 1 sig24 against the surge during the run, with more maker fills on the absorption side than on the exit side.
  • Lean toward the exit. For the limit orders an MM places in a 5-minute bin: the distance behind the best quote on the absorption side minus the same distance on the exit side, in bps, less the same MM's value in PRE2H. Positive means the MM places its exit orders relatively closer to the best quote than before the surge.

Classes (on the box)

  • Liquidation cascade when forced liquidations are at least 10% of the box's taker USD; otherwise big price move when the mid moves at least 168 bps from the box's start to its end; otherwise no big price move. 47, 314 and 561 surges.

The price patterns (15 minutes after the run; a different bar from the classes)

  • Peak move. The largest mid move from p0 in the surge direction up to t1 + 15 min. Final move is the move at t1 + 15 min, and share kept is final over peak.
  • Big-move bar g. The 95th percentile of the peak move among runs of the same length (153 bps for runs up to 15 minutes, 195 for 16 to 30, 240 for 31 to 60, 320 for longer).
  • Patterns. Small price move (peak below g); held (share kept ≥ 0.75); partly reverted (0.25 to 0.75); reverted (below 0.25); against (the final move is a move of at least g against the surge).
  • Quintile cut points. −84, +6, +78 and +191 bps of R(120), about −$0.32, +$0.02, +$0.27 and +$0.66 per HYPE. Q3 (+6 to +78 bps) moved little.
  • Net taker flow after the run. Surge-side taker USD minus the other side's, in 5-minute bins after t1; negative when it runs against the surge.

Key results

  1. A surge shocks six times as many MMs as the same clock time on a quiet day.

    5.9% of active-MM rows are shocked in the surges and 1.0% in the matched control windows. 90% of the shocked MMs in surges moved against the flow, as a maker filling the surging takers would (Section 2).

  2. In a typical surge, a few of about 100 active MMs take on a large position.

    About 19 absorb inventory and 4 are shocked; 91% of surges shock at least one MM (Section 2).

  3. The price mostly keeps its move for 15 minutes after the run.

    7% of surges give back more than three quarters of their peak move, and 2 of 922 end with a big move against the surge (Section 3).

  4. How far the price comes back over two hours goes with the taker flow right after the run, and not with the MMs' positions or quotes.

    The MMs absorb the same 27% to 36% of the surge in every reversal quintile and quote the same way. In one regression of R(120) that also includes the MMs' absorption, shock size, lean and cancels, only the taker flow in the 15 minutes after the run has a coefficient distinguishable from zero (Section 3).

  5. MMs close their positions when the price comes back and keep them when it does not.

    Two hours after the run, the MMs in Q5 hold none of their surge position; in Q1 they still hold a position equal to 23% of the surge's taker volume (Section 3).

What the surge does to the spread, the depth and the best quote is on the companion page, What a one-sided surge does to the book.

How the event set is built, step by step
  1. Taker flow per bin. HYPE taker buy and sell USD per 30-minute bin, data/redef_imb_measures_30m.parquet.
  2. Side score and surge flag. The robust 7-day score above; the $25,000 floor binds in 0 of 21,923 bins per side. Cutoffs are the full-sample 94.25% quantile of each side's score.
  3. One-sided test and box. Flagged one-sided bins of the same direction merge into a box, one unflagged bin allowed between two flagged ones.
  4. 5-minute duration run. Around the box, the run of 5-minute bins in which the surging side's 5-minute score is at or above its 90th percentile (3.33 buys, 3.51 sells).
  5. 1-minute run. Inside the duration run plus 5 minutes on each side, the strongest run of minutes above the stay threshold that reaches 3 times the 6-hour 95th percentile at least once. 63 events qualify only at 2 times, and in 32 no run qualifies and the duration run is used.

Builders code/redef4_events.py (selection) and code/redef4_steps.py (duration run, anchors, catalogue). Catalogue tables/redef4_events_F1_one_sided.csv.

2. Who takes the inventory

A median 100 MMs are active in a surge, about 19 absorb inventory and a median 4 are shocked. In 91% of surges at least one MM is shocked.

  • A surge shocks six times as many MMs as the same clock time on a quiet day.

    5.9% of active-MM rows are shocked in the surges and 1.0% in their matched control windows.

  • In surges, shocked MMs almost always move against the flow; in quiet windows the direction is a coin flip.

    90% of shocked MMs in surges changed position the way a maker filling the surging takers would. In the control windows, scored against the direction of the matched surge, the share is 48%.

  • The five largest absorbers in a surge are usually not shocked.

    They take a median 30% of the surging side's taker USD, but only 25% of them are shocked. Their normal position swings are among the largest in the surge (the 94th to 95th percentile of sig24), so a large trade is a small multiple of their sig24: 84% of the unshocked ones never reach 3 sig24 at any minute of the run.

Set-up problem. The top-five numbers and Table 4 use the active-MM population before the rule of at least 1 limit order per minute was added (112,425 surge rows). The 5.9%, 1.0%, 90% and 48% above use the current population (97,869 rows).

Event minus control is that share in the event less the share in its own control, over event-control pairs. Active MMs, sig24, dq: see the symbols list. Sources tables/review0917_fam_summary.csv, tables/review0917_fam_pairs.csv, from code/review0917_fam_reduce.py over data/redef2_pop_parts_all_{F1of,F2,F2box,famctrl}act (built 2026-09-17 by code/review0917_fam_pop_build.py) and data/redef2_pop_parts_all_orderfixact for the net-flow column. The shocked share of all rows is the share the median per-window share does not show: large windows carry more rows. Population: active MMs with a HYPE fill in PRE2H and in the window and a post-only fill in PRE2H, without the order floor of the population of record; the population of record is rebuilt for F1, its controls and the net-flow boxes but not for the F2 events, so the table stays on one rule.

    In the 5 minutes after t1, net taker flow runs against the surge in every quintile: a median $1.2m in Q5 and about $0.5m in each of Q1 to Q4. In Q4 and Q5 it stays against the surge for about 35 minutes; in Q1 to Q3 it is near zero from minute 5 on.

    The active MMs' combined position change over the run is 27% to 36% of the surge-side taker volume at the median in each quintile, with 18 to 21 absorbers and 4 or 5 shocked MMs.

  • MMs do not lean their quotes further toward the exit when the price comes back.

    In the first 10 minutes after the run, the median lean toward the exit is +0.11 bps in Q1 and −0.17 bps in Q5, on orders placed about 10 bps behind the best quote. Exit-side orders are half of the size MMs place in every quintile.

  • MMs close their positions as the price comes back.

    At t1 the MMs hold a position equal to about 27% of the surge's taker volume in both Q1 and Q5. Two hours later Q5 holds 0% and Q1 still holds 23%.

  • The 15-minute price patterns split on the same flow.

    Over the 15 minutes after the run, the median held surge takes another $0.43m of net taker flow in the surge direction, and the median reverted surge meets $3.16m against it.

What this does not establish
  • R(120) and the flow after the run cover the same window. They move together partly by construction, and nothing here says the flow came first.
  • The lean differences are small against the quoted distance. Every lean difference across quintiles is under 0.3 bps, on orders about 10 bps behind the best quote. MMs do not lean the reversal way; the lean may still differ from zero.
  • Q1 and Q5 both hold the largest surges. The peak move and the surge volume are highest in Q1 and Q5, so a return in bps mixes surge size with direction. Quintiles on the surges above g alone, and a fixed cut at −100, 0, 100 and 250 bps, order every result the same way. Quintiles of the return over the peak move are not yet built.
  • Every comparison is descriptive.
