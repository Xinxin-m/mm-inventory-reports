Exp8 — 10 roster MM2026-09-04

Descriptive, not causal. MMs appear by volume rank only.

Results 2026-09-23 · HYPE label, ten coins of orders, every perp coin of fills · 2025-03-27 to 2026-06-30 · Source: Exp8 analysis.md, section 65.10 · All reports

The 306 clause-(d) wallets, one by one: market makers or algorithmic traders?

We read, one by one, the 306 wallets that the daily MM label admits only through its clause (d).

  • Who. 306 wallets labeled HYPE market maker (MM) on at least one day, 2025-03-27 to 2026-06-30, never through the label's main clauses.
  • How. Each wallet is classified twice: by a fixed rule on its order and fill statistics, and by an AI reader that sees the same statistics without the wallet's identity.
  • Answer. 261 are algorithmic traders, 3 are MMs, 19 are contested between MM and algo, and 21 are retail.

Key results

  1. Almost all of them are algorithmic traders, not market makers.

    261 of 306 are algo; 3 are MMs on both reads, with 10.6M of volume (Section 2).

  2. They make as often as MMs, but one side at a time.

    Maker share 0.74 for the algo wallets against 0.82 for true MMs; a buy and a sell go out in the same minute on 0.009 of their quoting minutes against 0.72 (Section 3).

  3. Their order mix looks like an MM's, which is why clause (d) catches them.

    Post-only orders are 0.85 of an algo wallet's orders and 0.98 of a true MM's (Section 4).

  4. They trade in short sessions at a low rate.

    5.0 hours in the market a day and 6.8 orders per hour in the market, against 24 hours and 1,514 orders for true MMs (Section 3).

  5. A both-sides test would replace clause (d) without an order floor.

    "Quotes both sides within 30 bps of the mid" keeps 83% of true MMs' MM days and 3% of these wallets' clause-(d) days (Section 6).

TO DECIDE: three kinds of wallet that the rule and the reader cannot settle

  1. A program with frontend clicks: an MM operator who also clicks, or a person placing post-only orders by hand? 77 of the 306 mix frontend market orders with program activity: 47 alternate frontend days and program days, 11 change once, 19 mix both within a day.
    • Most of their flow is a program. Median order gap 3.8 s, a quarter of their gaps under 1 s, frontend market orders 1.3% of orders.
    • The order data cannot say which post-only order was clicked: the web app can send post-only orders too.
    • True MMs mix as well: 39 of 120 have 3 or more days with a frontend order inside a program day.
    • Current: the label follows the program (MM or algo); retail only where frontend orders are 30% or more of a day.
  2. One-sided makers (①, ②, ③; 146 wallets, $622.8M of volume): MM or algo? They fill as maker (0.86) and fill 26% of their post-only orders, against 1.2% for true MMs, which cancel and requote. But they quote one side at a time (two-sided minute share 0.005 against 0.72) and place 38 orders a day.
    • The algo label is not only takers. Matched algo wallets are 0.06 maker, but the whole algo class is 0.90 maker at the median, mostly through GTC orders.
    • Options: algo (current), MM, or a separate one-sided-maker class.
  3. Grids far from the mid (④; 21 wallets, $16.9M of volume): MM or algo? They rest both sides (0.50 of the time) about 20 hours a day, but a median 100 bps from the mid, so they rarely set the best price.
    • Options: algo (current), or MM with a flag.

1. The MM label and the 306 wallets

The daily MM label (v5d). For each wallet and day on HYPE, the label tests four clauses in the order (c), (a), (b), (d); the day is MM if one holds. A post-only order can only rest on the book, never take. Frontend orders come from the Hyperliquid web app.

  • (c) 2,000 or more post-only orders that day.
  • (a) 1,000 or more post-only orders over today and yesterday, 70% or more of all orders.
  • (b) 200 or more post-only orders today, 70% or more of orders, two orders under 1 s apart, frontend orders under 1%.
  • (d) Active on 5 or more of the last 14 days. Over the last 5 active days: post-only 80% or more of orders, the day's shortest order gap averaging 1 s or less, frontend under 1%. No minimum number of orders.
  • Second pass. A day that fails all four stays MM if the previous HYPE day was MM and the wallet's post-only orders on all coins stay high.

The 306 wallets ((d)-only). Of the 3,130 wallets labeled MM on HYPE on at least one day, the 306 whose every MM-labeled day comes from clause (d) or the second pass, never from (a), (b) or (c).

Comparison groups.

  • True MM (120 wallets). Drawn at random from the 972 wallets whose MM days come mostly (half or more) from clause (c).
  • Algo, frontend and retail wallets. HYPE wallets never labeled MM, grouped by their most common label: algo, retail through the frontend clause, or retail with no frontend orders.
  • Matched. Only wallets that trade about as often as the 306: 11 to 44 HYPE orders per active day and 15 or more active HYPE days (the 306's middle half).

2. How each wallet is labeled

The rule (first match wins; ten coins, whole window).

  • Too few: under 50 orders.
  • Program: half or more of its active days are program days (frontend and TP/SL under 10% of orders, and half the order gaps under 10 s or post-only orders living under 10 s). Or: frontend and TP/SL under 10%, and a median gap of 10 s or less, 12 or more hours with orders on the median day, or 500 or more orders a day.
  • MM: a program with 25% or more of its volume on coins where it quotes both sides near the mid: median quote within 30 bps, and a bid and an ask resting together half the time, or a buy and a sell placed in the same minute on 20% of its quoting minutes.
  • Algo: any other program.
  • Retail: not a program, and frontend or TP/SL orders 10% or more, or frontend days 30% or more.
  • Unclear: the rest.
Where each threshold comes from
  • 12 or more hours and 500 or more orders a day: the bot signals of the relabeling study (Allium, section 8.5). A buy and a sell in the same minute on 20% of minutes: its market-maker test.
  • Frontend 30% of the day's orders: the label's retail clause.
  • 10 s gaps and order lives, 10% frontend, 0.5 resting share, 25% of volume and 50 orders: set for this study.
  • 30 bps: set after the reader's verdicts were in. It gave 15 wallets the rule calls MM and the reader does not; a looser version gave 23. Agreement on the MM class is therefore not an independent check.

The reader. An AI agent receives a prompt that describes the three kinds of wallet and one unlabeled wallet's activity statistics, and guesses the label.

  • The three kinds, as the prompt describes them. MM: a program that quotes both sides at once near the best prices and ends the day near flat. Algo: a program that is not market making, such as a one-sided post-only buyer, a grid, or a taker bot. Retail: a person clicking, with frontend market orders, TP/SL orders and long irregular gaps.
  • Blind. Four agents each read about 107 wallets: the 306 mixed with 120 wallets from the comparison groups. None sees the address, group or current label.
  • Output. A label (MM, algo, retail or unclear), a confidence, and a one-line reason.
What the reader is given for each wallet
  • Activity: orders, active days, first-to-last active days, orders per active day.
  • Time in the market: hours in the market per day; mean, median and longest run of consecutive active hours; orders per hour in the market.
  • Order gaps: p5, p25, p50, p75, p95 of the seconds between consecutive orders; share of orders in the same millisecond as another.
  • Order types: post-only, GTC, IOC, frontend market and TP/SL shares; days with a frontend order; client order id share; share of orders at the wallet's five usual sizes.
  • Order life and distance: post-only cancel share, life and distance from the mid; GTC life and distance; share of post-only orders within 10 bps.
  • Quoting and fills: two-sided resting share, limit buy share, volume, maker share, one-way share, turnover, end-of-day position over the largest position, typical position size.
  • Day counts: frontend, program, two-sided program, slow, light and mixed days.
  • Coins: active coins, and the top three coins with their maker, two-sided and frontend shares.

The final label. Where the rule and the reader agree, that label. Where the rule is unclear, the reader's label. Where the rule says retail only because of TP/SL orders sent by a program, algo. Where one of the two says MM, "MM, contested".

4. The 261 algo wallets and their orders

Each algo wallet falls into one of six kinds.

  • ① One-sided maker, directional (80). Maker share 0.5 or more, one side at a time, and most of the day's fills in one direction. It builds or unwinds a position with post-only orders at the best price.
  • ② One-sided maker, one round trip a day (59). As ①, but it buys about as much as it sells each day (one-way share 0.3 or less) and trades under 4 times its largest position: it opens a position and closes it.
  • ③ One-sided maker, repeated round trips (7). As ②, turnover 4 or more. The closest to an MM, but still one side at a time.
  • ④ Two-sided, far from the mid (21). Both sides on a coin, but more than 30 bps from the mid: a grid.
  • ⑤ Mostly taker (41). Maker share under 0.5.
  • ⑥ Rule undecided, reader: algo (53). The rule said unclear (too slow for its program test) or retail (TP/SL orders sent by a program); the reader saw post-only orders, fixed sizes and client order ids.

6. A test to replace clause (d)

The test. Over the wallet's last 5 HYPE order days: a bid and an ask resting together half the time, or a buy and a sell placed in the same minute on 20% of quoting minutes; and a median post-only quote within 30 bps of the mid. A clause-(d) day that fails becomes algo.
