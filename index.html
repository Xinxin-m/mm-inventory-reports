Market-maker inventory events on HYPE

How HYPE market makers absorb and unwind a burst of one-directional taker flow, measured against the whole labelled market-maker population. Start with the Exp8 page. The storyline page reads the same evidence as one chain.

Key results

Flow is absorbed broadly; a few already-large MMs end the event shocked.
The median event has 115 MMs with absorption-side maker fills and 5 shocked MMs. The five already held larger positions before the event: median |position| 1,752 HYPE against 254 for the rest. Inside one wallet their resting size was close to even across the two sides (§4.3). (§4)
Section 4 →
The wallets that absorb the most flow are usually not the wallets the shock rule flags.
In 38.4% of events none of the five biggest absorbers (the MMs with the most absorption-side maker USD) is shocked. Their position change is a median 48.9 times that of the event’s median other MM, in HYPE. Their own position scale is also the event’s largest, so the change is only 2.2 times that scale. For 73.9% of them the position never reaches 3 times the scale at any minute. (§8)
Section 8 →
Matched controls turn several apparent responses into ordinary market making.
915 control windows at the same clock times carry the same measurements. They show no quoting lean to one side, no inventory move of shock size and no quoting shift after the window. (§4)
Section 4 →
Across all active MMs, position scale predicts inventory shocks; trading activity does not.
A wallet’s shock rate (how often it is shocked across events) has Spearman correlation +0.65 with its median |position| before events and +0.08 with its lifetime trades per bin, every date included. Much of the +0.65 is mechanical: the position scale in the shock rule has a fixed dollar floor. The +0.08 has no such issue. (§5.4)
Section 5.4 →
Equity is flat at the median and wide in the tails.
Equity is the whole account value. The median change for shocked MMs is +0.13% at event end and +0.15% at +6 h. 10.2% of shocked MMs, counted once per event, are below −5% at +6 h, every date included. Snapshots are 2 to 5.5 hours apart. (§4.7)
Section 4.7 →
A large sweep and the lasting price change are a second apart.
A sweep is one taker order that fills through several price levels. The second that holds a $1m sweep ends near where it began. The price moves in the next second, once the best bid and ask have been refilled with small size. (§6.6)
Section 6.6 →
Revert shocks recover fastest and step shocks hardly at all
The evidence is Table 77. Recovery times, minutes after the peak, on the Exp8 — Unwinders page.
Section 11.3 →
In the first 5 minutes after the peak, the measure of record, fast unwinders lean toward the exit and slow unwinders lean away
On the Exp8 — Unwinders page.

Exp8 — What a one-sided surge costs the book →

Section 64, on the 922 one-sided taker surges. The spread widens to 1.8 times its own 12-hour baseline at the flow peak and is still 6% wider an hour after the surge ends, 35% after a liquidation cascade. The taker-surge storyline →

One flow chart in the grammar of the draft paper's Figure 1. A surge of very large taker orders moves the price and sweeps the resting orders. Nearly every MM absorbs some, and the inventory lands on a few large, busy MMs. They back the absorbing quote off and unwind passively, at a speed that is a trait of the MM. The equity effect is small. Every number carries its event set and the experiment behind it.

The story so far →

How to read these. Everything here is a descriptive association; no causal claim is made. The two studies use different event definitions and should not be pooled. The 2026-09-04 report detects events on ten wallets, and its counts describe those ten. The redefinition page detects events on market-wide taker flow and reads the whole labelled MM population against them.
